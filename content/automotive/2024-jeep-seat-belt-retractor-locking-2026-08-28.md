# 2024 Jeep seat-belt retractor locking

## Bottom line

A vehicle-sensitive emergency locking retractor can temporarily prevent a belt from being pulled farther out when the vehicle slows, turns, accelerates, or is tilted. This normal motion-sensitive behavior should not prevent slack from winding back into the retractor.

Passenger seating positions in the checked 2024 Jeep Wrangler, Gladiator, and Compass materials also use a switchable Automatic Locking Retractor (ALR) for installing a child restraint. Pulling the belt fully out engages ALR; it then clicks and permits inward retraction while preventing further extension. Unbuckling and allowing the belt to retract completely resets it to the normal emergency-locking mode.

Therefore, a belt that genuinely will not retract inward is not explained by either normal locking mode. Safe first checks are to park level, unbuckle, remove tension, inspect for a twist or obstruction, and slowly feed a small amount inward. If it still does not retract completely, Jeep says a belt assembly whose ALR or other function is not working properly must be replaced. Do not lubricate or dismantle the mechanism, and do not use that seating position until inspected.

If the complaint concerns the harness built into a child safety seat rather than the Jeep's vehicle belt, follow the child-seat manufacturer's instructions; the Jeep's motion-sensitive retractor is not involved.
