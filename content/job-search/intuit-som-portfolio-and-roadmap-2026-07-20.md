## Evidence-backed chronology

1. **TY22 Supercharge Front Doors** established the customer-first thesis: reduce SKU-name burden, personalize relevant features, and simplify navigation. The broader portfolio reported $52 million in incremental revenue, but the current source does not support treating that as Luis's personal result.
2. **Reductive GTKM/PI** scaled to more than 80% of Catchall traffic. EOS evidence reports 109 index on revenue per auth and 101 index on auth-to-intuit-start, with an $8.7 million forecast. Paid and aspirational mix increased, while start conversion faced pressure.
3. **Ask to Start** tested guided complexity and confidence questions. Four of 13 routes won, producing approximately $1 million in incremental revenue. Homepage reach was too small, so the team moved the concept to a higher-volume product-and-pricing surface. The scaled approach improved mix and revenue but reduced A2S and S2C.
4. **Divergent New Start** reduced the burden of early SKU choice and right-sized final price. V1 produced 105 index on auth-to-complete. V2 produced 122 index on auth-to-start. All recipes reduced aspirational rates below 6%, while the revenue-per-auth gap remained.
5. **FY24 ownership** placed Reductive GTKM/PI V2 and V3, Start with Docs, Catchall rules-based personalization, and related onboarding work inside Luis's Catchall/new A2I and Onboarding A2I lane.
6. **FY25 planning** extended the strategy into Pay for What You Use, upgrade attach, rightsizing, price transparency, delayed authentication, delayed SKU selection, document-first entry, dynamic journeys, expert-led starts, and mobile/cross-device onboarding. These are planning artifacts, not proof that every item shipped.
