# Intuit SOM Divergent — Why This Attempt Succeeded

## Executive answer

This attempt succeeded because Luis did not begin with the most controversial structural change. He first simplified the existing onboarding flow and found conversion and revenue improvements. That work identified which complexity signals created value and built economic room for the larger change. He then removed the inherited requirement that customers select a final SKU before TurboTax understood their return. The team fixed the platform edge cases that had weakened earlier attempts and let actual tax complexity determine the final product and price.

## The two linked changes

### 1. Reductive GTKM

The first change simplified the existing onboarding flow. The team tested and reordered complexity signals, moved PY Prep after Start, stopped losing recipes early, and scaled combinations that improved conversion and revenue without increasing aspirational mix.

This stage did two jobs:

- It created near-term conversion proof.
- It identified the economic and behavioral conditions required for a structural change.

### 2. Divergent New Start

The second change removed the requirement that customers begin onboarding in a final SKU. Customers could move through their actual tax complexity before TurboTax right-sized the final product and price.

This was controversial because the prior model treated product selection as an early commitment. Luis explains that this behavior was inherited from boxed desktop software, where buying the box determined the product. The archive does not explicitly document that origin. It does confirm that the start-SKU requirement was a platform constraint.

## Why earlier attempts struggled

The TY22 learning materials show that earlier changes often improved only one side of the business:

- Higher paid mix supported revenue but increased aspirational placement.
- More Free starts improved conversion but did not produce enough downstream conversion to offset revenue loss.
- New navigation and price-finder treatments improved parts of the funnel but retained early product assignment.
- Several recipes showed softness in start-to-conversion, aspirational mix, or revenue per visitor.

These efforts changed merchandising or funnel presentation. They did not remove the product-assignment constraint.

## Why this attempt was more successful

1. **It sequenced proof before disruption.** Reductive GTKM created conversion and revenue evidence before the team challenged the core commercial model.
2. **It changed the architecture.** Divergent removed the start-SKU requirement instead of only changing the way products were presented.
3. **It fixed implementation failures.** The team addressed refresh, mobile handoff, users remaining in Free, and finish-and-file downgrade edge cases before scaling.
4. **It delayed commitment until the system had evidence.** Product and price followed the customer's demonstrated tax complexity.
5. **It used multiple guardrails.** The team tracked starts, completion, revenue per acquisition, aspirational rate, and product-rightsizing signals.
6. **It iterated with discipline.** Losing recipes were stopped early. Promising recipes were revised and scaled after the team understood their effects.

Do not combine the percentage lifts with the index results. Do not claim complete revenue parity. Do not say that Intuit intentionally overcharged customers or violated regulations. Use customer-fit, price-transparency, and regulatory-risk language.
